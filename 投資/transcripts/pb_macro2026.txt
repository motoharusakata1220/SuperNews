タイトル: Why the Fed Can't Cut Rates Yet (And What It Means for Markets)

Today we're going to talk about why the Federal Reserve is stuck in a very difficult position and what this means for financial markets going forward. The current federal funds rate sits at 4.75% and markets are pricing in two cuts before year end. But I think the Fed faces a trilemma that makes cutting much harder than people realize. First there's the inflation problem. Core PCE is still running at 2.5% which is above the 2% target. While it's come down significantly from the 2022 peaks the last mile of disinflation is proving stubborn. Services inflation in particular remains elevated driven by shelter costs and wage growth. Second there's the employment picture. The labor market is remarkably resilient with unemployment at 3.9%. In a normal cycle you'd expect some loosening by now but we're seeing continued job creation. This is great for the economy but it means the Fed doesn't have the typical cover of rising unemployment to justify cuts. Third and this is what most people miss there's the fiscal picture. The US government is running a deficit of roughly 6% of GDP. This fiscal expansion is effectively stimulative which works against the Fed's tightening efforts. So what does this mean for investors? I think we're looking at a higher for longer rate environment. The implications are that growth stocks with distant cash flows face continued valuation pressure while value stocks and companies with strong current earnings should outperform. Fixed income investors should consider shorter duration bonds. And for equity investors generally this is a stock picker's market not an index fund market.
