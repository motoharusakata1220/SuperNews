India is on track to become the world's fourth largest economy by nominal GDP this year potentially overtaking Japan. But how did this happen and what does it mean for the global economic order? Let's break this down. India's GDP is expected to reach $4.18 trillion in 2025 compared to Japan's $4.23 trillion. With India growing at 6.8% and Japan at just 1.8% the crossover is essentially inevitable. Several factors are driving India's remarkable growth. First demographics. India has the world's largest population at 1.44 billion with a median age of just 28 compared to Japan's 49. This young workforce is a massive advantage in terms of productivity and consumption growth. Second the digital transformation. India's Unified Payments Interface processed over 14 billion transactions in a single month last year. The combination of Aadhaar digital identity and affordable smartphones has created a digital infrastructure that's arguably more advanced than many developed countries. Third manufacturing momentum. The Production Linked Incentive scheme has attracted significant foreign direct investment particularly in electronics and semiconductors. Apple now manufactures over 14% of its iPhones in India and this share is growing rapidly. However there are real challenges. Income inequality remains severe with GDP per capita of only $2,500 compared to Japan's $34,000. Infrastructure gaps particularly in logistics and power distribution constrain industrial growth. And the quality of employment remains a concern with much of the workforce in informal sectors. For investors the key takeaway is that India represents one of the most compelling structural growth stories in the world but selectivity is crucial. Large cap quality stocks and infrastructure plays are likely the best way to gain exposure.
