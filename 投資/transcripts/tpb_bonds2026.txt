If you're an investor who only focuses on stocks you're missing half the picture. The bond market is actually much larger than the stock market and right now it's sending some very important signals about where the economy is headed. Let me explain what you need to know. A bond is essentially an IOU. When you buy a government bond you're lending money to the government and they promise to pay you back with interest. The yield is the annual return you get. Currently the US 10-year Treasury yield is around 4.2% which is historically quite attractive. This means you can get a 4.2% annual return with essentially zero credit risk. The yield curve which shows the relationship between short-term and long-term interest rates has recently un-inverted. This is significant because an inverted yield curve where short-term rates are higher than long-term rates has historically been one of the most reliable recession indicators. Now that it's normalized it doesn't mean we're out of the woods but it does remove one of the major warning signs. For portfolio construction I typically recommend that younger investors in their 20s and 30s allocate about 10-20% to bonds while those closer to retirement should have 40-60%. But the specific allocation depends on your risk tolerance and financial goals. The most important principle is diversification across asset classes not just across stocks.
